# Self-Initiated Demo: Evidence-First Research Brief Template

## 1. Decision / question
State the exact decision the research should support. Avoid researching a broad topic without a clear use case.

## 2. Scope
- geography
- target audience / customer
- time period
- key definitions
- exclusions

## 3. Executive summary
Three to five bullets covering the strongest findings, the uncertainty around them, and the practical implication for the client.

## 4. Evidence table
| Claim / finding | Evidence | Source | Date | Confidence / limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example | Short paraphrase, not copied text | URL / citation | YYYY-MM-DD | Why this may or may not generalize |

## 5. Key findings
For each finding:
1. state the finding in one sentence;
2. show the evidence;
3. separate facts from interpretation;
4. note counter-evidence or uncertainty;
5. explain why it matters to the project.

## 6. Recommendation options
Present practical options with trade-offs rather than pretending uncertain evidence is definitive.

## 7. Slide conversion map
- Slide 1 — title / decision
- Slide 2 — executive summary
- Slide 3 — market / context
- Slide 4 — finding 1
- Slide 5 — finding 2
- Slide 6 — finding 3
- Slide 7 — options / trade-offs
- Slide 8 — recommendation / next steps
- Appendix — sources, methods, extra data

## QA checklist
- Every numerical claim has a source.
- Publication date and event date are not confused.
- The research population matches the client's target population.
- Old evidence is not presented as current without caveat.
- Quotes are short and necessary; paraphrase is preferred.
- Contradictory evidence is acknowledged when material.
- Slide headlines communicate conclusions, not just topics.

> This is a self-initiated template showing delivery structure, not a client project.
